# Signal: signal-cli, the text styles and speech without markup

## Layout

- `signal_io` (signal-cli child, JSON-RPC, outbound allowlist; a text's styles checked before they go)
- `signal_text` (markup to Signal's text styles at send time: plain text plus `textStyle` ranges in UTF-16 units; `spoken`: the same reading for speech; `unlinked`: outside text in code's notes without its links, a name's real endings in `web_endings`)

## Conventions

- Signal shows Markdown as typed ("**bold**" with its asterisks). Every text goes out through `App._deliver`, which turns markup into Signal's text styles (`signal_text.styled`: **bold**, ~~strike~~, `code` and closed fences as monospace, a "# heading" line bold, "- " and "* " line starts "• ", *italic* only with clear edges; never underscores, a web address (punctuation after it is the sentence's), math such as 2*3 or 2**3, a closing marker after a slash or a backslash (a glob: "**/node_modules/**"), a lone or unbalanced marker, or markers across lines; a line where a dropped marker would sit inside a web or mail address of the result ("**evil**.com") goes out as typed) and `SignalClient.send` sends them as signal-cli's `textStyle` ("start:length:STYLE", UTF-16 code units; one bad entry fails the whole send in signal-cli, so a list that `signal_text.valid` refuses is dropped and the text goes out without). The stored text keeps its markup (history, search, a resend after a restart converts again); a conversion that fails sends the text as written; a footer goes out in italics. The guards read what the phone shows too: brain's link guards also read the text without markers (`signal_text.unmarked`) and defang or drop a link only that reading has, where it stands; `claims` reads every text as `claims.shown` (no markers, no list or heading start: "**Reminder set** (#5)" is code's own note's look); `initiative.check_text` runs each check on both forms; a calendar place loses its markers before its links. Code's notes that quote outside text (the digest's titles, places, senders, summaries and subjects, the calendar watch's titles, the music note's names, a Maps link's label, the echo of an audio file, the page's title in the /learn reply) leave every link out, never shown and never defanged (owner, 21 Oct; `signal_text.unlinked`, through `calendar_tools.clean_note` where there is a cap): a reading wider than `_LINKISH` (which keeps `styled`'s guard and so her answers as they are), in four patterns, each run on its own so a candidate one refuses never hides another's link: `_ALWAYS` (any scheme with "//" wherever it starts, "1-https://", "²https://", also percent-encoded; a mail address as Android reads one, any character outside ASCII in its parts, any letters' ending; an address by its four numbers), `_WWW` and `_NO_SLASHES` (www. on any ending, and the schemes iOS links without "//": tel:, sms:, maps:, itms-services: and the others, in lower case only as iOS reads them, so "News:Today" stays; each when no ASCII letter or digit is right before it, so "hotel:" stays and "™tel:" goes) and `_NAMES` (a name with a part after its first on a real ending, `web_endings.ENDINGS`: IANA's root zone list of 21 Oct 2026 in its own scripts plus the endings Signal's apps still link, or any xn-- one; any letters' ending before a path, a query or a port), each with its port, path, query or the rest of a word after a dash or an underscore ("paypal.com-verify"). A name's part takes in what Signal Desktop takes in (marks and symbols: "नमस्ते.com", "C++.NET", "a°b.com"), and its ending is also read up to a letter NFKC made of a sign ("evil.com™") and up to where wide letters start ("evil.com中": iOS ends the link there); other scripts' full stops count as dots only before a Latin ending ("evil。com"; "会議。資料" stays; known gap: "例子。中国" stays, untested on a phone). Every reading a phone may see: as typed ("evil.com*1") and without markers ("**evil**.com"), in NFKC's letters (fullwidth "ｅｖｉｌ.ｃｏｍ"), the characters no app shows (format characters, variation selectors, U+034F) left out and read as spaces (iOS ends a link at a zero-width space), each also with a run of dots and a whole run of dots, underscores or dashes at a word's edge or after a dot read as spaces ("Wait...evil.ch", "_evil.com_", "a.-evil.com"; the dashes inside "xn--p1ai" stay); the markers inside a link and those that only wrapped it go with it, every other marker stays (her styling); an empty pair of brackets or quotes and a separator left at an end go, one space stays, and two words are never glued into one ("evil(ab.cd).com" leaves "evil .com"). The text is read again until no reading finds a link; after 4 rounds each word that still holds one goes whole, so the time grows only with the length (4,000 characters of nested pieces: 9 ms, 3 ms in clean_note at 255; 50,000 characters of an audio file's echo: under 0.2 s; before, the second re-review measured 63.5 ms at 255 and 622 ms for 3,205 characters of echo). It runs after the hidden characters go and before the cap, and again after it, as a cut can make a word that is no link into one ("evil.com1x" cut to "evil.com…"): `clean_note` reads at most 4 times the cap and leaves a word cut there out whole, so no cut leaves part of a link. After `unlinked` has removed what it recognises, a **structural safety net** (`signal_text.netted`, owner 21 Oct) runs in the note path only (`clean_note`, before the cap, and the uncapped echo) as the guarantee past what a phone's link reading catches, because it does not read links as a phone does: the text becomes what the user will receive — NFKC, and every character no app shows (`_unseen`) a space — and then, within each space-delimited word, a character that joins a link's parts becomes a space when a letter, digit or combining mark stands on each side: a dot or dot-like character (`_DOT_LIKE`: every Po full stop, dot or point in the Unicode data, so "evil。com", "evilｰcom" and "evil․com" all break), a colon or colon-like one, @ (broken whenever a local part stands before it and a domain after, so "bob'@host" goes too), a slash or backslash and their look-alikes, %, or a run of underscores. Kept for reading: a dot or colon between two digits (a price 4.99, a time 09:30, a version 1.2.3.4), a dot then one letter then a dot or the word's end (an abbreviation e.g., z.B., i.e., p.m.), and punctuation at a word's end. One left-to-right pass, so the time grows only with the length (16,000 characters of any shape: under 5 ms). Because the net breaks every link-joining character, the cap can only shorten a word it has already made safe, never cut one into a link. Checked on every note path with `tests/android_links.py` (Signal Android's own Linkifier and mail reading, ported); Signal Desktop (linkify-it with its endings) and iOS (macOS's NSDataDetector) only by hand, with probes on a Mac (the third probe, 21 Oct: 26,000 inputs through 12 note paths and all three oracles leave no tappable link). Known costs: the net breaks a name on no real ending too, so a title or subject loses a dot or slash there — "Mr.Smith" becomes "Mr Smith", and "report.pdf", "node.js", "and/or", "κ.λπ." and "Room 4.2/B" lose their joins; NFKC also folds a sign in the sent text ("™" becomes "TM", "①" becomes "1"). Prices, times, versions and one-letter abbreviations stay. `unlinked` on its own still leaves these as they were, for her answers (`brain.defang_links`) and any non-note reading. Known gaps: an ending IANA adds later only sharpens `unlinked`, never the net (the net is the guarantee); plain phone numbers stay tappable (the apps' own phone-number reading), by design. Gemma's calendar and mail tools keep their text (`tests/test_calendar_tools.py` and `tests/test_mail_digest.py` pin it as in 0.9.5). A text over 2000 UTF-8 bytes gets only the ranges inside its first 2000 bytes (signal-cli's body piece; the apps' handling past it is untested). Code-written texts use the same markup: bold headings, a blank line between sections, one item per line ("•", or the item's own number); Gemma's tool results (`mail_digest`, `list_reminders`) keep their lines, so her requests stay as they were. Speech never carries markup: `voice.spoken_text` reads through `signal_text.spoken` (a struck word is left out); links and paths are left out, but a command the user types (`voice.COMMANDS`, every one /help names) is said as "slash reminders". Three dots between two words theory's parser reads as chords are a comma (`voice._dots_between_chords`: "C... G... Am..." is "C, G, Am.", one part the voice service spells as one run; folded into a full stop, each chord was a part of its own and "G." was read "Jay", the chord speech audit, 18 Oct), and so are three dots between a chord and a joining word before another (`voice._JOINS`, and, or and then in English and the eight languages that spell chords: "G... then C" is "G, then C"; the review, 18 Oct); after or before other words they stay ("G... then the chorus"); "C...G...Am" without spaces is read the same way. A run of marks is said as its first, but a comma or a semicolon after a dotted abbreviation's last dot stays (`voice._one_mark`: "N.C., then G", "e.g., apples", and a table's "| N.C. | G |" as "N.C.; G."). `voice.sentences` ends a part at "N.C." only before a capital, as at "a.m.", and never before chords (`voice._chords_follow`: a word theory's parser reads, then another or the sentence's end: "N.C. G C D" is one part, "N.C. A good start." two; the review, 18 Oct). Her stage directions ("(pause)", "*smiles*", "(laughs)", "(pausa)", "（間）") are never spoken or sent (owner, 18 Oct): `voice.without_stage_directions` leaves out a whole bracketed group of only `language.STAGE_DIRECTIONS` and `STAGE_SOFT` words, or a starred one of `STAGE_STARRED`'s (third-person and -ing forms: "*smiles*", never "*pause*" or "*smile*"); one in lower case wherever it stands, a capitalised one only between sentences (`_between_sentences`: a title in brackets stays, "(Applause) by Lady Gaga"; known gap: German's "Also (Pause) weiter" is said); never bold ("**Beat** the eggs"), code (a fence or inline, matched first and kept) or a bracket right beside a letter or digit ("time.sleep(pause)"; review 18 Oct), keeping the punctuation around it ("Em... (pause) ... C..." is "Em... C...") and every other bracket ("C (live version)", "(x2)", a link's text); it runs in brain's filters (`_filtered`, practice replies too: the stored answer has none) and in `spoken_text`. Known gap: her own messages (`initiative`) are not filtered.

## Rules

- signal-cli runs with `--trust-new-identities on-first-use`, pinned in `signal_io.signal_cli_command` (never left to the default, never `always`); setup's own signal-cli calls pass the same. A changed safety number drops that sender's messages: signal-cli's receive `exception` (0.14.9: `{"message", "type": "UntrustedIdentityException"}` next to the envelope) reaches the bridge on its own stream (`SignalClient.problems()`, type, sender and device only, never the message, which names the account), is logged without the account and kept in `status.json` (`security.identity_changed`) until a message from that user opens again; doctor and `/status` show ✗ with the compare-then-trust steps (setup_assistant.md, 7. Operations).
- Her own Signal account's devices: counted with `listDevices` through the running JSON-RPC (`SignalClient.list_devices`: ids and creation times, never names) at the start and every 4 hours, kept in `status.json` (`security.linked_devices`); more than 1 (this Mac) is ✗ in doctor and `/status`, and one note when the count goes up.

## Gotchas

- One signal-cli process per account: stop the bridge before running signal-cli by hand.
- A photo arrives as one JSON line of base64 from signal-cli (20 MB → 27 MB): the reader limit is 32 MiB.
