# Web search, places, music, YouTube links and chords

## Layout

- `fetcher` (guarded web fetch)
- `video` (YouTube links: the strict link check, the YouTube helper run, a long video's parts and code's note before them, the last video kept in memory per account for 10 minutes, the check that her answer never copies the captions)
- `theory` ([chords]: the chord arithmetic, four pure functions with a fixed input and one frozen result out, `transpose`, `capo`, `notes`, `key`, each result with its `spoken` form for a call; `read` and `chord_of`, the one chord parser the tool and the check share; `parse_key` and `keys_in`, keys in words in 11 languages; standard library only, no network, files or programs; every rule is the chords rule book's, S1 to G3, kept outside the repo with the design)
- `chords` ([chords]: Gemma's `chords` tool around theory, `ChordTool` and its `Result` (code's fixed lines, the spoken line, the chords in and out, the page that holds them); `note` and `spoken`; `read`, the reader of chords in her words with her voice's context rules (ChordSpeller's for English, ChordWords' for the ten others, the packs' music words and joins: `MUSIC_WORDS`, `JOINS`, test-checked against the Swift packs; known gap: it keeps the gaps, marks and forms of before the chord speech fixes of 18 Oct, so a chord after ";", a spaced slash, "…", "‖", "⏸" or a mark, an "A D E A" row, "C-7" and a bracket's alterations are not checked); `check`, the reply check)
- `spotify_auth` (PKCE paste sign-in, tokens), `spotify` (Web API client), `soundtouch` (wake a Bose), `music_tools` (what Gemma's music tools do)
- `web_search` (Ollama web search, Brave as backup; counters, pauses)
- `maps` (Google Places and Routes, location pins, home area)
- `tools/youtube/`: the YouTube helper (youtube-transcript-api in its own uv environment; the captions of one video by its ID, from www.youtube.com only).

## Commands

- Web search keys (from the login keychain into `web.env`, 0600, lengths only): `scripts/set-web-keys.sh` (the person runs it in their own Terminal window: it may ask for the login password, and in her own user for the Mac password)
- YouTube helper: `cd tools/youtube && uv run pytest -q && uv run mypy` (recorded responses, no network)

## Rules

- YouTube links (`video`, `tools/youtube`): always on with read_link (no switch). Only a link in the user's current message is read (`video.video_id`: youtube.com, www., m. (watch, shorts, live, embed), music. (watch) and youtu.be; no login, port or other host; exactly one `v=` whose raw value is the ID), and only its 11-character ID reaches the helper, never the link; a video found by web search is named, never read. The helper (youtube-transcript-api 1.2.4 pinned, its requests stack in `uv.lock`) runs with fixed argv (`captions --until 7200 -- <ID>`: the ID last, after `--`, since about 1 in 64 IDs starts with `-`; the fake helper reads its argv with the real parser's shape), no shell, a clean environment; it asks only https://www.youtube.com (every request and redirect through `GuardedAdapter`, at most 3 redirects), with no proxy settings, .netrc, cookie file or account (`trust_env` off; the session lives for one run), stops itself after 20 s (the bridge kills it at `youtube_helper_timeout`, 30 s) and prints one JSON line: manual captions before auto-generated ones, each in the video's own language first (the auto-generated track's language, else the track YouTube shows by default, else English, then YouTube's order, which goes by language name: live, an English lecture without an auto-generated track first came back in Arabic; so a hand-written track in another language, often a translation, beats YouTube's automatic one in the video's own language), never YouTube's machine translation; or an error code, never YouTube's or the library's own error text. Nothing is transcribed (no yt-dlp, no Whisper): no captions is "This video has no captions." Captions are outside text with a page's flags (no remember or forget, no reminders, device changes wait for a yes, links defanged; the user's own video link stays tappable, `Turn.allowed_links`: `video.link_in`, the link as the user's message has it, never Gemma's argument, which a planted line could make carry another link) and never stored: no document, the tool log keeps `prompts.VIDEO_STUB`, learn_link and `/learn` refuse video links. At most 2 hours are read (`CAP_SECONDS`; a longer video's first 2 hours, said in code's note and in the result), and at most `CAP_CHARS` (120,000 characters; the helper's `MAX_CHARS` is the same, and it says `cut_by`): captions cut by size get their own words (`VIDEO_READING_SIZE`, `VIDEO_CUT_SIZE`: "the first 1 h 10 min of a …"), never "2 hours". Captions over `PART_CHARS` (15,000) are split into even parts of whole lines (a line about every 30 s, or sooner at `LINE_CHARS`, 2,000; no part over `PART_CHARS`: a part closes early, and a longer line is cut with its time on each piece): code's note goes first (`video.reading_note`, "Reading a 1 h 40 min video: about 1 minute.", in place of the busy note; `PART_SECONDS` from the evals: about 5 s a part on the M6 mini), then Gemma notes each part in a call of its own (no tools, the user's message in each) and her answer joins the notes. A second read_link of the same video in one answer reads nothing again (`Turn.videos_read`, `prompts.VIDEO_READ_ABOVE`: no part calls, no second note). The answer gives times for the main points, says when the captions were auto-generated and is capped by `reply_max_tokens` (600). It never holds the captions word for word, in code: `brain._without_captions` (`video.without_copies`) leaves out each sentence of hers holding 13 caption words in a row (case and punctuation aside, also across sentences; Chinese, Japanese and Thai by character), and, when runs of 6 or more hold more than 30% of the captions' words, every sentence with one; then code's line `prompts.VIDEO_NOT_SENT` gives the user's link. Asked for the full transcript, the prompt has her give the link. The last video's captions stay in memory per account and conversation for 10 minutes (`KeptVideos`; a timer drops them, `/new` too), never on disk: read_link with that video's link (any of its forms) reads them again without the helper, and while they are kept code's line `prompts.VIDEO_KEPT` (the link and the minutes left) is last in her prompt (live evals: without it she answered follow-ups from her own summary). When the user's current message holds a YouTube link (`video.first_link`, as `link_in` finds it), code's line `prompts.VIDEO_IN_MESSAGE` follows, last (read_link reads that video's captions; asked for the full transcript, the link, which opens the video: never called the transcript); a message without one gets no line and its request stays byte for byte as before (`test_without_a_youtube_link_her_request_is_the_one_from_before`; every non-video eval case's first request matched fa95b51's). read_link's description never names YouTube (see Gotchas). Never on a call (a link cannot be spoken; no line there either). Logs hold codes, character counts and times, never the ID or a title. `status.json`'s `youtube` part (always shown): a video without captions, unavailable or age-restricted is YouTube answering (working); a block is `refused`, no answer `no_answer`, a changed YouTube `bad_answer`; doctor reads the library's version from its files (no network) and warns when the helper is missing (in her own user its fix is the deploy, `prompts.DOCTOR_YOUTUBE_DEPLOY_FIX`; her own words name no command, `VIDEO_ERRORS["helper_missing"]` points to doctor). YouTube's terms forbid automated access: accepted by the owner for personal use (SECURITY.md, known gaps).
- Music (Spotify): text message only (Spotify's policy forbids voice control; call/voice turns are refused in code); after outside text in the same answer music may stop or get quieter but never start or get louder; nothing from Spotify is stored or learned; code's note names what plays without the links in its names (`calendar_tools.clean_note`, 200 characters; a public playlist's name is anyone's text), with Spotify's own link (`_link`: open.spotify.com only); volume never above the cap; the Bose wake talks only to configured private addresses.
- Web search: queries only to Brave and Ollama (keys only in `web.env`, 0600); results are data, never stored (tool-log stub, Brave's terms), their links only in a code-written Sources note; an answer that searched refuses `remember`/`forget` and its device requests wait for a yes; an answer that read mail, the calendar, the user's projects (any `project_*` tool: `Turn.read_project`) or documents (`Turn.read_documents`) cannot search; at most 2 searches per answer (1 on a call); Ollama first, Brave as backup (one request a second, counted per month), a service is paused after 429/401/402/403; Brave's top page only through the guarded fetcher; no cache; queries with an email address, an IBAN or a 9+ digit number are refused.
- Places and routes (Google): never after mail, the user's projects (`Turn.read_project`) or documents (`Turn.read_documents`) in the same answer; an event's place may be used; "near me" is the last Signal location share (kept as "here" in memory for 3 h, never logged; the message itself stays in the history like any message; a link inside a question is not a pin) or the home area rounded to ~1 km (the address only in the private config; it goes to Google to be looked up and for routes from/to home; the state table keeps the rounded centre and the address's tag, HMAC-SHA256 keyed with the Google Maps key (`maps.home_tag`: a new address or key means one lookup), and the day's failed lookup as that tag, never the address; the Directions link (sent and stored as a Sources note) names the rounded centre for a home end, never the address: none for a route home without a known centre, no origin from home; at start `maps.forget_home_address` rewrites or deletes rows an earlier version kept with the address and rewrites its Directions notes (`Store.rewrite_notes`: an UPDATE, so the search index follows, then its `optimize`), maps on or off, then writes the log back into the file); daily cap per API; results are data, never stored, links only in the Sources note (a place's name as its Maps link's label without links, `tools._link_label` through `calendar_tools.clean_note`).
- Chords ([chords], owner 17 Oct; off without the section: no tool, rules section or list line, every request byte for byte as before, `tests/test_chords.py` pins the sha256): the chord work is code's (theory.py), never Gemma's; one tool, `chords` (never `music_…`: that prefix is Spotify's handler, which refuses calls), offered on texts and calls, an instant tool, its description short and with no site or brand name (see Gotchas). Code's note carries the chords (`Reply.chords`, after the Sources note; in her history only as code's note, marked as quoting outside text after a page); she is told to say what was done and never write the chords out. A refusal of the user's own chord (in the message or a page) stays in code's note after a later call that worked, and that call's "Read as:" says it was left out ('"Hm7b" left out'; `chords.told`, eval of 18 Oct); a call of the same action that puts a chord of her own in its place is refused as a guess (`chords._guessed`, error `guessed`, never in code's note; review I4: her "Hm7" for "Hm7b" was worked out and, on a call, said with no word of a guess); any other refusal shows only when no call worked. A capo's key to sound in is `to_key`, as a move's target (`from_key` still read); a capo whose key to sound in is the shapes' own while the user's message names another key of the shapes' mode (a capo keeps the mode) gets a question back, never code's pick (`chords._other_key`; eval of 18 Oct, chords "G" with shapes "G" gave "Capo 0"; review I3: a pick took "the original recording is in A major"), and none when the key to sound in is one of the user's key words ("I sing it in G major") or the other keys are the shapes' own chords (known gap, eval `chords_capo_sing_in_g`, 18 Oct: with the recording's key and the singer's in one message she passed the recording's as `to_key` herself, 4 of 4 and 2 of 3 in a second phrasing; code cannot tell which is meant), and fret 0 is "with no capo", never "Capo 0". Key words in the user's own message win over her `from_key` or `to_key` (`theory.keys_in`, `theory.is_key`: never the article "a", never a do-re-mi word before a noun, "la menor idea"; her argument is compared in the naming the move uses, so "Bm" beside h-Moll is B♭ minor; several keys of a role: hers stays when it is one of them; a move's key to move to never by the key the chords are in now (`chords._keys_now`: the move's own source key, or the estimate's best, with their relatives: "this song is in G major; put it in Bb" gave "No step") unless hers misreads it; a capo's key to sound in only when hers misreads one of them, another key of the same letter or sound (`chords._misreads`: "B" for H-Dur), since a capo's message may name the song's key too: review C1, "in G major ... 3 semitones higher" with her Bb gave "no capo"); a chord page's "Key:" line is read by code from the web result that holds the chords. The reply check (`chords.check`) runs whenever the tool ran or a source holds chords: with the tool, each chord she names must be one its results name or were given (root as spelled and ending); without it, a root the sources have (the user's message, the turn before from the store, the web results, a call's earlier results); her answer is read whole (`chords.read(lines=True)`: a list's lines join one run, a key in words and "B flat" join runs, three names joined by slashes are a list; an English word counts only as ChordSpeller reads it, so never "Do", "Go", "Ah"), and each sentence or line of a run with another chord is left out; after a result that names frets (a capo's, a capo sheet's, a move's "Or:" line), so is each one with a fret she names that neither it nor a source names (`chords.frets_in`: a number after a fret word of the conversation's language or English, or an ordinal before one, "4. Bund", whose full stop ends no sentence; a fret in words is not read; review C2, "Capo im 4. Bund" beside code's Capo 3 in all 4 runs); nothing of hers left after a chords call that worked: `prompts.CHORDS_DONE`; on texts a skipped tool gets one nudge (`prompts.CHORDS_NUDGE`), on a call none. The reader works out each sentence once, so its time grows in line with the text (16,000 characters of her answer, 4,000 of a source). On a call the result is said after her words, then "The chords come in a message after the call."; after the call `CallManager._after` asks `Host.call_chords` (after the dropped and unfinished notes, before the summary) and code sends one message with every result of the call and its source, only when a transpose ran; the summary goes through the check. Chords from a page are data: after a web search the tool log keeps a stub for the arguments and the result. Logs: action, a count, the time and an error code, never chords, keys, song names or sources. Guards (theory): 64 chords, 1,000 characters, 12 characters an ending, no digit run made a number. Its cost in every request, measured with web search and calls on (`tests/test_chords.py` pins it): 1,743 characters on texts (about 440 tokens), the rules' 616 and the tool's 1,127, and 1,566 on calls (about 390), above the design's estimate of 265 and 225; the call gate above (`--ab call_goodbye 40`, 18 Oct): passes 23 of 40 in both arms (p = 0.589), missed hang-ups 5 without the tool and 8 with it (one-sided Fisher p = 0.27), so the tool stays on calls; 40 tries per arm cannot rule out harm (about 300 per arm would detect that gap, or 12 misses or more with it would reach p < 0.05), and both arms share the live hang-up bug (fake tool tags written as text, junk after end_call): fix that first, then run it again with 100 or more per arm, judged on missed hang-ups.
